# Bibleberry | AI Department Handoff Standard

**Status:** Accepted organizational standard (merged in PR #294, November 22, 2026)  
**Authority:** Aaron Waid, Project Owner  
**Applies to:** All permanent AI departments within the Bibleberry development organization  
**Repository:** `Bibleberry/Bibleberry`  
**Document path:** `docs/ai-operations/handoff-standard.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

Bibleberry operates through multiple specialized AI departments. Each department may be represented by a succession of AI conversations as context windows become lengthy or operational circumstances change.

A conversation is temporary. **The department is permanent.**

This standard establishes a consistent process for transferring responsibility between outgoing and incoming conversations without losing important operational information, altering departmental authority, or requiring each successor to reconstruct the department's history.

The objective is reliable continuity with minimal administrative overhead.

**Quick access (copy-and-paste instructions):** [Outgoing department message](#111-message-to-the-outgoing-conversation) · [Incoming department message](#112-message-to-the-incoming-conversation). Both messages are universal; neither requires editing a department name or charter path. The outgoing conversation supplies those details in its handoff.


## 2. Two Components of Departmental Continuity

Every departmental transition relies on two distinct sources.

### A. Permanent Department Charter

The permanent charter defines the department's:

- Mission and standing responsibilities.
- Authority and restrictions.
- Relationships with other departments.
- Sources of truth and governing documents.
- Standard operating responsibilities.
- Department-specific continuity requirements.

Department charters are maintained under:

`docs/ai-operations/departments/`

These documents apply across all conversations occupying that department.

**An outgoing agent must not independently rewrite or reinterpret its departmental authority during a handoff.**

Charter changes require Aaron's approval and an appropriate repository change.

### B. Temporary Operational Handoff

The operational handoff describes the department's current working state at the moment a conversation is replaced.

It records recent activity, decisions, active matters, unfinished obligations, and immediate next actions.

Operational handoffs are generated by outgoing departmental conversations and provided to their successors by Aaron.

They do not replace the department charter or GitHub's authoritative engineering records.

Routine handoffs do not need to be committed to the repository.

## 3. When a Handoff Occurs

Aaron determines when a departmental conversation should be replaced.

Typical reasons include:

- The conversation's context window becoming lengthy.
- Declining reliability or continuity in the existing conversation.
- Completion of a major work period.
- A deliberate operational reset or transition.

A department must also be capable of preparing a handoff whenever Aaron requests one.

**The outgoing agent should preserve continuity rather than initiate additional work during the transition.**

A handoff request does not authorize new development, repository changes, assignment dispatch, or organizational changes.

## 4. Required Operational Handoff Content

Every departmental handoff must include the following sections.

### 4.1 Department Identification

Identify:

- Department name.
- Permanent charter path.
- Handoff date and relevant time zone.
- Outgoing conversation's operational responsibility.

Do not reproduce the entire permanent charter.

### 4.2 Current Operational Summary

Provide a concise description of the department's current situation.

Explain what the department has been working on, where that work stands, and whether any material concerns require attention.

This section should orient the incoming agent without becoming a historical narrative.

### 4.3 Work Completed During the Conversation

Identify meaningful work actually completed.

Distinguish among:

- Investigation or analysis completed.
- Recommendations presented.
- Decisions approved by Aaron.
- GitHub or other system changes actually performed.
- Work delivered but awaiting further verification or acceptance.

Do not represent recommendations, proposed changes, unmerged PRs, or reported tests as accepted results.

For material actions, include relevant references.

### 4.4 Decisions and Authorizations

Preserve significant decisions Aaron made during the conversation.

Identify:

- The decision.
- Its scope.
- Whether implementation or execution was authorized.
- Any conditions, restrictions, or unresolved approvals.

**A recommendation made by an AI is not an authorized decision unless Aaron approved it.**

Distinguish established policy from temporary exceptions and proposed improvements.

### 4.5 Active and Unresolved Matters

Identify work that remains unfinished or requires future attention.

For every Issue-backed assignment, including engineering, planning-originated repository work, documentation, governance and administration, use its **permanent two-word codename and originating GitHub Issue number/link**.

For each matter, include:

- Permanent codename and Issue reference, where applicable.
- Current verified state.
- Next responsible person, department, or agent.
- Relevant dependency or blocker.
- Immediate next action.
- Supporting GitHub references when available.

Include unresolved material incidental findings under [AI Development Protocol §5A](../ai-development-protocol.md#5a-incidental-findings-and-engineering-knowledge-preservation): durable Issue/PR references or sanitized restricted-record references, current disposition, evidence checkpoint, and accountable next action. Flag any preservation blocker; the handoff itself does not replace the durable record.

If there is no outstanding work, state that explicitly.

Do not invent tasks to fill this section.

### 4.6 Verification Checkpoint

Record when and how important operational information was last verified.

Where relevant, include:

- Repository and last inspected `main` commit SHA.
- Active Issue and PR links.
- Branch names and last confirmed pushed commit SHAs.
- Actual review or CI status.
- External service or environment status, if independently checked.

Distinguish verified facts from agent reports, assumptions, and information that may have changed.

A historical checkpoint must never be presented as continuously current.

### 4.7 Immediate Next Actions

Give the incoming conversation a short, prioritized account of what to do first.

Each action should identify its purpose and any prerequisite.

Do not automatically authorize work merely by listing it as a next action.

Where Aaron has not approved execution, specify that the next step is investigation, recommendation, or awaiting authorization.

### 4.8 Department-Specific Continuity

Include the specialized information required by the permanent department charter.

Examples:

**Development Management**

Current assignments, permanent codenames, implementing agents, dependency sequencing, review queue, next available work, shared-file ownership, and dispatch authorization.

**Independent Review**

Assignment codenames, PR and Issue identities, exact reviewed HEAD SHAs, existing review dispositions, blocking findings, correction status, and verification limitations.

**General Administration**

Current organizational initiatives, special assignments, unresolved operational questions, decisions, and relevant cross-department dependencies.

Additional departments must define their specialized handoff requirements in their own charters.

## 5. Source-of-Truth Rules

GitHub remains Bibleberry's authoritative shared engineering record.

The hierarchy of information must be respected:

- Approved product specifications define intended product behavior.
- The canonical development roadmap governs engineering sequencing.
- Accepted repository code establishes the implemented baseline.
- GitHub Issues establish assignment identity, authorized scope, and ownership.
- Pull requests, reviews, commits, and CI runs establish submitted work and verification evidence.
- Department charters define permanent organizational responsibilities.
- Operational handoffs preserve temporary conversational continuity.

A handoff is a guide to the authoritative records, not a competing source of truth.

Material decisions or engineering state that must survive beyond a chat should be recorded in the appropriate existing GitHub Issue, PR, or governing document when authorized.

Do not create duplicate tracking systems solely for handoff purposes.

## 6. Assignment Identity and Two-Word Codenames

Bibleberry uses a **mandatory two-word codename system** for all human-directed assignments that change repository files or produce Pull Requests, **not just engineering**. Engineering Planning candidates also receive codenames when created. Read the cross-department identity-first gate in [AI Development Protocol §2B](../ai-development-protocol.md) before beginning repository mutations.

The system exists primarily to help Aaron recognize, remember, and coordinate assignments while moving rapidly between multiple AI platforms, coding agents, and departmental conversations.

Codenames are not decorative labels. They are a core part of Bibleberry's operational coordination system.

### 6.1 Registry allocation

Use the accepted [Codename Registry](codename-registry.md) for every authorized new codenamed Issue. It governs allocation, availability and historical recycling.

### 6.2 Assignment continuity

An assignment retains its original codename. When a codename is recycled, the originating Issue number distinguishes the new assignment from earlier ones. Include that Issue number in every formal handoff.

### 6.3 Relationship between codenames and GitHub Issues

The codename provides the primary human-readable identity.

The originating GitHub Issue provides the permanent authoritative assignment record.

For example:

**Steady Finch | Issue #269**

The codename and Issue number refer to the same assignment, but serve different purposes.

- **Codename:** Allows Aaron and the AI departments to recognize and discuss the work.
- **Issue number and link:** Establish the authoritative assignment identity, scope, ownership, evidence, and history.

Neither identifier replaces the other.

### 6.4 Consistency across the organization

The same codename must be used consistently throughout an assignment's lifecycle.

This includes:

- GitHub Issue titles and assignment records.
- Related Pull Request titles and descriptions.
- Coding-agent handoffs and implementation reports.
- Independent Review reports and dispositions.
- Checks & Merge handoffs.
- Development Management coordination.
- Operational handoffs between departmental conversations.
- Cross-department discussions about the assignment.

An assignment Issue should ordinarily be titled:

`[Steady Finch] Correct Shelf Rollover Behavior`

A corresponding PR, including documentation and administrative PRs, must preserve the same codename:

`[Steady Finch] Implement Shelf Rollover Correction`

Branches and formal handoffs should also follow the naming requirements in the AI Development Protocol.

**Cross-department preflight:** Before any department or agent creates a new branch, edits repository files or opens a PR, verify the authorized Issue, codename, ownership and scope; create the originating Issue first under the [Codename Registry](codename-registry.md) if authorized and none exists. Never exempt a request merely because it came from Planning, Admin or an informal conversation. Read-only advice does not by itself require a new assignment. Reuse an existing Issue/codename for follow-up work, and do not invent a second tracking record. Automated dependency PRs from the expressly named bot senders are exempt from human-assignment identity, but not review/merge controls. The required PR check is described in [AI Development Protocol §2B](../ai-development-protocol.md).

### 6.5 Codenames survive agent and conversation changes

**An assignment's codename does not change when its personnel change.**

An engineering, documentation or administrative assignment may pass between agents, undergo review and corrections, require replacement PRs, or continue across several departmental conversations.

It retains its original codename throughout.

Do not generate a new codename because:

- A different model takes over implementation.
- The original coding conversation is replaced.
- A reviewer requests corrections.
- A PR is replaced or revised.
- The assignment moves between departments.
- The work is temporarily blocked or deferred.

Multiple PRs associated with one originating Issue retain that Issue's permanent codename.

An older Issue without a codename must not be arbitrarily renamed. Preserve its established identity unless Aaron authorizes a change.

### 6.6 Required assignment identification in handoffs

Whenever an operational handoff discusses an Issue-backed assignment, including non-engineering work, identify it using:

**Codename | Issue number | PR number (if applicable) | Current status**

For active implementation or review work, also include:

- Responsible coding agent or department.
- Relevant branch.
- Last verified pushed commit SHA.
- Review or verification state, where applicable.
- Next responsible party and action.

For example:

`Copper Meadow | Issue #283 | PR #286 | In Progress (under Review)`

The example identifies the required format; actual handoffs must report the assignment's currently verified status.

### 6.7 Governing principle and existing policy

This section reinforces the mandatory assignment identity requirements in:

`docs/ai-development-protocol.md`, Section 2B.

The handoff standard must remain consistent with that protocol.

**Codenames identify the work. Issue numbers establish its official record. Agents and conversations are temporary.**

The assignment's identity and history persist even when the AI systems working on it change.

## 7. Accuracy and Evidence Requirements

Outgoing agents must:

1. Distinguish completed, in-progress, proposed, blocked, and unverified work.
2. Use permanent two-word codenames and originating Issue references when discussing all Issue-backed assignments, engineering and non-engineering.
3. Provide exact references for material GitHub activity when available.
4. Identify the latest verified commit when code or review state matters.
5. Disclose checks that were skipped, failed, not run, or only reported by another agent.
6. Preserve unresolved disagreements and approval requirements.
7. Avoid inventing missing facts, authorizations, links, or verification evidence.
8. Clearly identify information that may be outdated.

If live GitHub or another required system cannot be inspected, say so. Preserve the last confirmed state and indicate what must be rechecked.

Never include credentials, access tokens, private keys, or sensitive operational secrets in a handoff.

## 8. Incoming Conversation Procedure

When a new conversation receives a departmental handoff, it must:

1. Read the supplied operational handoff to identify the claimed department and charter path; do not assume the claim is authoritative.
2. Verify that department's charter and the four-department responsibility map on accepted `main`, then read the charter and governing documents.
3. Recheck current GitHub state before acting on time-sensitive engineering information.
4. Identify material discrepancies between the handoff and live evidence.
5. Preserve the established assignment codenames and Issue identities, including any subsidiary function/resource the department owns.
6. Provide a read-only activation report and stop for Aaron's direction. A handoff alone does not authorize fresh repository work.

The incoming conversation does not need to repeat completed investigations without cause.

However, it must not assume that an outgoing agent's observations remain current, particularly where other agents may have continued working.

**A handoff transfers context. It does not independently grant authority.**

## 9. Handoff Quality and Length

Handoffs should be complete enough for operational continuity but no longer than necessary.

Prioritize:

- Current responsibilities over historical background.
- Decisions over lengthy discussion transcripts.
- Verified references over unsupported summaries.
- Unresolved obligations over completed routine activity.
- Actionable next steps over speculative future possibilities.
- Consistent assignment identities over informal descriptions that could cause confusion.

Do not reproduce entire Issue bodies, PR discussions, specifications, or repository documents when a reference and short explanation will suffice.

Do not copy the department charter into the handoff.

Do not omit important context merely to satisfy an arbitrary word limit.

The standard should reduce the amount of context each successor needs, not replace one oversized conversation with an oversized handoff.

## 10. Standard Operational Handoff Template

All departments should follow this basic structure, adding specialized sections when their charters require them.

### Bibleberry Department Operational Handoff

**Department:**  
**Permanent charter:**  
**Handoff prepared:**  
**Verification checkpoint:**  

#### 1. Current Situation

Brief summary of the department's operational state.

#### 2. Completed Work

Meaningful work completed, decisions implemented, and supporting references.

Use permanent codenames and Issue numbers for all Issue-backed assignments, including non-engineering work.

#### 3. Decisions and Authorizations

Aaron's decisions, approved actions, restrictions, and outstanding approvals.

#### 4. Active and Unresolved Matters

For each Issue-backed assignment, engineering or non-engineering:

**Codename | Issue number | PR number (if applicable) | Status**

Include the responsible agent or department, dependencies, blockers, next action, and relevant links.

For other matters, use their established project or organizational identity.

#### 5. Verified Technical or Operational State

Current verified repository, PR, Issue, environment, or other applicable evidence.

Include exact commit SHAs where relevant.

Identify uncertainties and the last inspection time.

#### 6. Department-Specific Continuity

Information required by the department's permanent charter.

#### 7. Immediate Next Actions

A concise prioritized sequence for the incoming conversation, with authorization boundaries clearly identified.

#### 8. Warnings or Important Context

Only material lessons, risks, unusual circumstances, or restrictions that the successor would not reliably discover from permanent documentation.

## 11. Standardized Conversation Transition Messages

These are the two **canonical instructions Aaron copies into ChatGPT** whenever a permanent AI department is moved from one conversation to another. They standardize the *request and activation*, while §10 standardizes the *operational handoff document* produced between them.

**Four departments only:** General Administration includes Checks & Merge; Engineering Issue Planning includes Project Board Stewardship; Development Management manages coding-agent resources; Independent Review is the separate technical-review department. The subsidiary functions and resources transfer with their parent department and do **not** require independent departmental charters or separate department-activation prompts.

**No manual substitutions:** Neither message contains a department name, charter path, Issue number or current commit to fill in. The outgoing conversation must supply its own department identity and exact charter path in the handoff. The successor discovers and verifies them from that handoff and accepted GitHub records.

**Use in order:**

1. Copy the complete **outgoing message** (§11.1) into the retiring department conversation. Receive the full handoff and retain it.
2. Start a new conversation and paste the complete **incoming message** (§11.2), followed by the unaltered handoff in the same message or the next message. Do not replace the universal instructions with the handoff alone.
3. Review the incoming conversation's read-only activation report. Its report is not permission to begin or resume repository mutations; Aaron separately gives any needed direction.

### 11.1 Message to the outgoing conversation

Copy **only** the content inside the block.

~~~text
# Bibleberry | Departmental Operational Handoff Request

I am preparing to replace this conversation with a new conversation taking over the same permanent Bibleberry department.

Prepare a complete operational handoff under the accepted standard in:
docs/ai-operations/handoff-standard.md
Repository: https://github.com/Bibleberry/Bibleberry

Your permanent departmental charter governs your responsibilities and department-specific handoff requirements.

Your assignment:

1. Read the accepted handoff standard and your permanent charter. Identify your department and include its exact charter path in the report.
2. Reconcile your current work against authoritative GitHub evidence where accessible. Preserve the last verification checkpoint and clearly flag anything you cannot independently verify.
3. Summarize the department's actual operational state: meaningful completed work, outstanding assignments, decisions, authorizations, blockers, dependencies, and next actions.
4. For each Issue-backed assignment, whether engineering or non-engineering, preserve the permanent two-word codename, originating Issue, relevant PRs, ownership, branch and exact verified SHA when applicable.
5. Distinguish merged and accepted work from open PRs, proposed work, review dispositions, actual CI evidence, assumptions, and unverified service status.
6. Preserve material context or decisions from this conversation that a successor cannot reliably recover from permanent documents and GitHub records.
7. Identify the responsible party and any separate authorization needed for each next action.
8. Include all department-specific requirements from your permanent charter.

Produce one self-contained "Bibleberry Department Operational Handoff" using the template in Section 10 of the handoff standard. Finish with a brief Successor Verification Checklist specifying the facts the incoming conversation must check independently.

This is a read-only transition assignment. Do not start new work; create or modify Issues, PRs, branches, files or Project records; dispatch agents; alter permanent authority; or merge or deploy anything.

After delivering the handoff, stop.
~~~

### 11.2 Message to the incoming conversation

Copy **only** the content inside the block. Then paste the outgoing handoff after it, either in the same message or immediately afterward. **There are no department names or paths to edit.**

~~~text
# Bibleberry | Departmental Activation and Continuity Verification

You are assuming responsibility for an existing permanent department of the Bibleberry software development program.

Repository: https://github.com/Bibleberry/Bibleberry

I am supplying the complete operational handoff prepared by your predecessor, in this message or immediately after it. Do not begin your activation assessment until the handoff has been supplied.

This is a read-only initialization and continuity verification assignment.

Initialization requirements:

1. Read the predecessor's handoff in full. Identify the claimed department name and permanent charter path from it; do not assume the handoff itself establishes authority.
2. Retrieve that charter from accepted main and confirm the department appears in docs/ai-operations/department-responsibilities.md. If the identity/path cannot be verified or conflicts with accepted documentation, report the discrepancy and stop rather than inventing a role.
3. Read the permanent charter, docs/ai-operations/handoff-standard.md, docs/ai-operations/department-responsibilities.md, and other governing documents required by the charter.
4. Independently verify the current main commit and all relevant active Issues, PRs, review dispositions, dependencies, authorization records, and outstanding work. Do not mistake dated handoff observations for current evidence.
5. Reconcile the predecessor's handoff against accepted repository records. Identify stale claims, missing context, unresolved responsibilities, missing evidence, and matters needing my direction.
6. Preserve existing assignment codenames, Issue/PR identities, departmental boundaries, and authorizations. Do not create duplicate assignments.
7. Provide an activation report covering documents reviewed, the verified repository checkpoint, your responsibilities and limits, active/unresolved matters, material discrepancies, immediate priorities, and any required decisions.

Restrictions: Do not create or modify branches, repository files, Issues, PRs, Project records, or service environments; do not dispatch agents, run implementation work, merge, or deploy. An outgoing handoff transfers context, not new authorization.

After presenting your activation report, stop and await my direction.
~~~

**Missing handoff:** If the incoming conversation has not received the predecessor's report, it must ask for that report instead of guessing its department. If GitHub is unavailable, it must disclose the limitation and cannot claim repository-verified activation. A handoff-only message without this initialization instruction is not the standardized activation procedure.

**Maintenance:** These two messages are stored here in the same authoritative document as the handoff requirements. Changes to them are organizational-policy changes, requiring Aaron's authorization and the normal Issue/PR/review process. Individual departments must not maintain competing prompt versions.

## 12. Organizational Ownership

Aaron retains authority over departmental responsibilities, organizational changes, and adoption of this standard.

General Administration coordinates development and reconciliation of permanent departmental charters and the common handoff procedure.

Individual departments may recommend improvements based on operational experience.

No department may independently expand its authority, redefine another department's responsibilities, or change the standard as part of an ordinary chat transition.

Changes to permanent organizational documents should follow the repository's established review and approval procedures.

## Governing Principle

**The department's responsibilities remain stable. The conversation's working context is transferred. GitHub preserves the authoritative record. Aaron retains control.**

Bibleberry's permanent two-word codenames ensure that engineering and non-engineering repository assignments remain recognizable across departments, models, and conversation replacements.

A successful handoff allows a replacement conversation to continue its department's work accurately, promptly, and without unnecessary reconstruction of the past.
